02 · CONVICTION LEVELS BY ASSET BUCKET
● High conviction — treat as firm value
CoreWeave Lease
·CoreWeave has faced repeated attacks on their creditworthiness and come out stronger every time. Their finances have improved, not deteriorated, under scrutiny — treat the lease as durable.
·CORZ had real delivery delays. CRWV didn't walk — they defended the partnership even as they themselves were attacked for choosing CORZ. That loyalty under pressure is a strong signal of commitment.
·CRWV has now spent a substantial portion of their capex credits into the buildout. Sunk costs lock them in. Meaningful billing revenue should start materializing as the remaining MW come online over the next two quarters.
● Medium conviction — valuable but unproven
Unleased Portfolio
·Market lease rates have improved significantly since the original CoreWeave deal — a new customer announcement at current market terms ($155–185/kW vs. $100/kW) would produce a meaningful re-rating of the stock.
·But management has been signaling a new customer for over a year. The continued absence of a signed deal is becoming a concern. "Actively evaluating" starts to sound like "can't close" after 12+ months.
·The 65% deal probability in our base model reflects this skepticism. The value is real and large — it's just not in the bank yet, and that distinction matters for where you hold vs. sell.
● Low conviction — optionality only
New Site / Footprint Expansion
·The Pecos 1.5 GW expansion is a genuine positive — scaling an existing site with secured land and contracted utility power is real, concrete optionality and deserves a place in the model.
·But I don't have high conviction in CORZ's ability to win entirely new power contracts. Every operator in the US is competing desperately for scarce grid capacity — CORZ has no structural advantage in that race vs. better-capitalized competitors.
·The BTM solution is intriguing as a way to bypass interconnection queues, but the economics of the generation source are unknown. The street will give some credit here — I give it less. The 70% probability / $5.5B NPV in our base case is already generous given the uncertainties.
